# Summary

## What this paper does

This study uses five CMIP6 large ensembles to identify drivers of summer Arctic sea-ice extent from 1970–2060. Candidate drivers include winter sea-ice volume, Arctic 2 m air temperature, Atlantic and Pacific ocean heat transport, with additional tests for winds, circulation, and SST.

For each model member, the ensemble-mean trend is removed and LK is calculated over the entire member time series. The information-flow estimates are then averaged across members and tested for significance. Surface air temperature is the most consistent driver; winter sea-ice volume is the next major precursor, while ocean-heat-transport effects vary by model.

## What covariance it uses

Covariance is across time within each member. The ensemble supplies repeated realizations and uncertainty reduction; it is not collapsed into current-time covariance. Short observational/reanalysis series are treated as indicative rather than strong model-evaluation evidence.

## Why it matters for IcyAlert

This paper is almost a direct blueprint for the initial IcyAlert variable set and CMIP6/SMILE use. It also justifies EC-Earth3 as one relevant ensemble source without making it the only possible process model.

## Limitation

The main calculation is approximately stationary after detrending and uses Arctic aggregate metrics, not dynamic gridded filtering.
