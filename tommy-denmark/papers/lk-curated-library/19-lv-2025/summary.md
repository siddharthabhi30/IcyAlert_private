# Summary

## What this paper does

Lv et al. use in-situ drift observations, reanalysis support, an Arctic surface-mixed-layer heat budget, and LK to study sea-ice thickness/concentration, mixed-layer temperature, and seven heat-source terms. They use bivariate LK for sea ice versus mixed-layer temperature and multivariate LK for the heat-source system.

The paper finds region-dependent two-way relationships. Near Fram Strait and northern Svalbard, Atlantic-water influence and vertical turbulence are important. In the Nansen and Amundsen basins, sea-ice melt more strongly drives mixed-layer changes, with vertical turbulence and ice-ocean heat forcing important.

## What data/covariance it uses

LK is calculated from observational time series along drifting trajectories. This is not a forecast-ensemble or assimilation study. The use of actual drift data makes it highly relevant for choosing physical sea-ice/ocean variables.

## Why it matters for IcyAlert

It supplies a modern, physically motivated candidate variable set beyond only 2 m temperature and sea-ice concentration. It also shows why causality may differ by Arctic region.

## Limitation

Coverage is sparse and trajectory-specific. The local full-text dump is complete, but the open Wiley PDF endpoint returned HTTP 403 and still requires manual download.
