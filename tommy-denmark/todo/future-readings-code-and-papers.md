## 1. Causal Spatiotemporal Dynamics & Information Flow (Liang–Kleeman)

### A. Spatiotemporal Graph Attention with Liang–Kleeman Flow

#### Causality-Aware Spatiotemporal Graph Attention Modeling for Multi-Station Storm Surge Forecasting Under Typhoon Forcing
- **Venue**: *Coastal Engineering* (2026)
- **Authors**: Coastal Engineering & Hydrodynamics Research Group
- **Links**: [ScienceDirect S0378383926000748](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378383926000748) | [ScienceDirect S0378383924000607](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378383924000607)
- **Abstract**:
  > Multi-station storm surge forecasting along complex coastlines is traditionally constrained by empirical statistical models or computationally intensive numerical hydrodynamic simulations. Purely data-driven deep learning models often act as black boxes, learning spurious correlations rather than true physical hydrodynamic interactions. In this paper, we propose a Causality-Aware Spatiotemporal Graph Attention Network (TGAT / CSTGNN) for multi-station storm surge forecasting under extreme typhoon forcing.
  > 
  > We utilize Liang–Kleeman information flow theory to quantitatively identify directed, asymmetric causal dependencies between observational stations and meteorological drivers. The derived causality matrix defines the directional connectivity of a dynamic graph. Graph Attention Networks (GAT) then dynamically assign attention weights across causally verified edges to capture spatial wave and surge propagation along the coast. Experimental results across major historical typhoon events demonstrate superior multi-step forecasting accuracy, physical consistency, and interpretability compared to unconstrained deep learning baselines.

### B. Time-Varying Liang–Kleeman Causality

#### A Time-Varying Causality Formalism Based on the Liang–Kleeman Information Flow for Analyzing Directed Interactions in Nonstationary Climate Systems
- **Venue**: *Journal of Climate*, 32(21), 7521–7537 (2019)
- **Authors**: Daniel Fiifi Tawia Hagan, Guojie Wang, X. San Liang, Han A. J. Dolman
- **DOI**: [10.1175/JCLI-D-18-0881.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-18-0881.1)
- **Abstract**:
  > The identification and quantification of cause-and-effect relationships among interacting components are essential for understanding climate system dynamics. While standard Liang–Kleeman information flow provides a rigorous, physics-based metric of causality for stationary systems, real-world climate interactions are inherently nonstationary and time-evolving. 
  > 
  > This study develops a time-varying Liang–Kleeman (TvLK) causality formalism to evaluate transient and shifting directed interactions in nonstationary climate systems. By integrating localized covariance estimation (via sequential Kalman filtering / localized window formulations) with information flow equations, the proposed framework tracks time-dependent causal rates without requiring asymptotically long stationary records. We demonstrate the framework on synthetic benchmark models and real-world nonstationary phenomena, including air-sea-land interactions and soil moisture–temperature feedback loops, establishing TvLK as a robust diagnostic tool for transient geophysical processes.

### C. Causal Graph Neural Networks

#### GL-LFGNN: Global-Local Dual-Branch Causal Graph Neural Network Based on Liang–Kleeman Information Flow
- **Venue**: *arXiv:2407.13289* (2024)
- **Authors**: Causal AI & Complex Systems Group
- **Links**: [arXiv:2407.13289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13289)
- **Abstract**:
  > Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have shown remarkable potential in modeling complex spatiotemporal physical systems. However, most GNN architectures rely either on static pre-defined Euclidean graphs or fully connected attention graphs, which frequently propagate non-causal noise and degrade out-of-distribution generalization.
  > 
  > We present GL-LFGNN, a dual-branch global-local causal graph neural network that leverages Liang–Kleeman information flow to establish rigorous causal constraints. The local branch uses LK information flow to construct an asymmetric physical adjacency matrix, restricting message passing strictly along verified causal pathways. The global branch simultaneously captures macroscopic system trends. By fusing local causal interactions with global representations, GL-LFGNN significantly reduces error accumulation over long prediction horizons on complex spatiotemporal benchmarks.

### D. Causal Attention in Transformers

#### ARCausal: Causal Mask in Transformer via Transfer Entropy for Multivariate Time Series Forecasting
- **Venue**: *Conference on Neural Information Processing / Time Series Track* (2026)
- **Authors**: Time Series & Causality Research Group
- **Abstract**:
  > Standard self-attention in Transformers allows every variable token to attend to all other tokens, leading to quadratic computational complexity and susceptibility to spurious temporal correlations. In multivariate time series forecasting, discovering the underlying directed causal graph is critical for robustness.
  > 
  > We propose ARCausal, a Transformer architecture that integrates transfer entropy into self-attention. Directed causal dependencies are estimated to construct a causal adjacency prior. We introduce both hard causal masks (pruning non-causal attention keys) and soft entropy-weighted attention biases. Empirical evaluations across financial, meteorological, and traffic benchmarks show that causally masked attention improves forecasting accuracy by up to 28% over unconstrained Informer and PatchTST architectures while offering transparent causal explanations.

## 2. Machine Learning Data Assimilation (ML-DA): Foundations, SOTA Directory & Abstracts

### 2.1 High-Level Training Paradigms in AI Data Assimilation

Modern AI weather data assimilation is structured across four primary methodologies:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        The AI Data Assimilation Spectrum                               │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Supervised "Imitate the Analysis" DA                                                │
│    • Observations + Background Forecast ──► Neural Network ──► Gridded Reanalysis     │
│    • Exemplars: FuXi-DA (npj 2025), FuXi-Weather (Nat Comm 2025)                       │
│                                                                                        │
│ 2. Generative-Prior / Diffusion DA                                                     │
│    • Training: Gridded Reanalysis (ERA5/HRRR) ──► Learn Prior Dynamics p(x)           │
│    • Inference: Prior Score ▽ log p(x) + Obs Likelihood p(y|x) ──► Posterior p(x|y)    │
│    • Exemplars: ReGen (JAMES 2025, PhysicsNeMo), Appa (arXiv 2025), DiffDA (ICML 2024) │
│                                                                                        │
│ 3. Latent-Space Classical DA                                                           │
│    • Training: ERA5 ──► Autoencoder Latent Manifold z                                  │
│    • Inference: Variational / Bayesian Optimization directly inside z                  │
│    • Exemplar: Latent DA / LDA (Science Advances 2026)                                 │
│                                                                                        │
│ 4. Direct Observation-to-Observation World Models                                      │
│    • Training: Raw Heterogeneous Obs ──► Spatial Latent z ──► Future Raw Obs           │
│    • Inference: Query Decoder for arbitrary variables (T2m, MSLP, Radiance)            │
│    • Exemplars: ECMWF GraphDOP (arXiv 2024, 2025), Google WeatherNext 3 (arXiv 2026)    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Detailed Paradigm Breakdown

1. **Supervised "Imitate the Analysis" DA**:
   - **Mechanism**: Directly maps background forecasts paired with sparse observations to gridded operational reanalyses (ERA5). FuXi-DA adds forecast loss supervision so the resulting analysis remains dynamically stable when passed to downstream forecast emulators.
   - **Characteristics**: Fast, deterministic point-estimate mapping; explicitly distills upstream 4D-Var choices and inherits reanalysis smoothing/biases.
2. **Generative-Prior / Diffusion DA**:
   - **Mechanism**: The model trains unconditionally on complete atmospheric state analyses (e.g. HRRR or ERA5) to master the prior distribution $p(x)$ of dynamically physically consistent atmospheric states. At inference, observations enter exclusively as a likelihood constraint $p(y|x)$ to steer diffusion reverse sampling without retraining.
   - **Characteristics**: Preserves fine-scale structures (e.g., gust fronts, convective gradients); enables zero-shot conditioning on arbitrary observational geometry; provides calibrated posterior ensemble spread.
3. **Latent-Space Classical DA**:
   - **Mechanism**: Trains a nonlinear autoencoder on multivariate global reanalysis to compress physical fields into a lower-dimensional latent representation $z$. Classical Bayesian or variational assimilation (3D-Var / 4D-Var) is then executed inside $z$ rather than the raw model grid.
   - **Characteristics**: Latent manifolds capture nonlinear flow-dependent physical balance; bypasses empirical covariance tuning while retaining variational optimization principles.
4. **Direct Observation-to-Observation World Models**:
   - **Mechanism**: Trained directly on massive historical archives of raw, heterogeneous observational sensor streams (satellites, weather stations, radiosondes, aircraft, ocean buoys) without requiring any gridded reanalysis (ERA5) ground truth:
     $$\text{Raw Observations } y_t \xrightarrow{\text{Encoder}} \text{Spatial Latent Grid } z_t \xrightarrow{\text{Transformer}} z_{t+k} \xrightarrow{\text{Query Decoder}} \text{Future Observations } y_{t+k}$$
   - **Latent Structure**: In ECMWF GraphDOP, $z_t$ is an approximately $1^\circ$ global spatial grid where each point holds a learned 1024-dimensional continuous feature representation. The network spontaneously discovers physical structures (jet streams, synoptic fronts, cloud formations).
   - **Decoupled Query Decoding**: At inference, the decoder can query the continuous latent workspace for any requested variable:
     - *"What is the 2m temperature at station coordinate $(lat, lon)$?"*
     - *"What brightness temperature does FY-4B AGRI channel 9 observe here?"*
     - *"Evaluate 850 hPa wind across a regular global grid."*
   - **Operational Infrastructure Realities**:
     - ECMWF IFS Cycle 41r2 (which created ERA5) is closed/licensed and non-reproducible.
     - OpenIFS 48r1 (released 5 March 2026 under Apache-2.0) provides the modern forecast model only (DA/adjoint code excluded).
     - ECMWF **Anemoi** is open-source under Apache-2.0 (`ecmwf/anemoi`), providing datasets, training pipelines, model components, and operational inference tools powering the Artificial Intelligence Forecasting System (AIFS).
     - Official GraphDOP model weights/code remain internal to ECMWF research; open third-party reimplementations exist.

#### The Two Research Frontier Poles

```math
\begin{aligned}
\text{\bf Pole A (Generative Score-Based DA):} \quad & p(x|y) \propto \underbrace{p_\theta(x)}_{\text{Diffusion Prior (ERA5)}} \cdot \underbrace{p(y|x)}_{\text{Obs Likelihood}} \\
\text{\bf Pole B (Direct Obs-to-Obs World Model):} \quad & y_{t+k} = \operatorname{Decoder}\left(\operatorname{Transformer}\left(\operatorname{Encoder}(y_t)\right)\right)
\end{aligned}
```

- **Generative DA (Appa / ReGen style)**: Leverages existing gridded reanalyses to learn the unconditioned physical prior distribution $p(x)$, and solves the inverse problem at inference via score guidance. Requires less raw sensor curation, handles arbitrary forward observation operators $H(x)$, and generates calibrated posterior distributions.
- **Direct Observation Learning (GraphDOP style)**: Skips the gridded reanalysis entirely. Trains end-to-end on raw observations to discover latent Earth representations directly from nature, eliminating all dependence on numerical 4D-Var pipelines.

## 3. Atmospheric Process Models & Forecast Evolution: Pure Learned, Generative Diffusion, and Hybrid Physics-AI

### 3.1 The Process Model Spectrum

In numerical weather prediction and Earth system modeling, the **process model** (or forward forecast operator $\mathcal{M}$) advances the state from time $t$ to $t + \Delta t$:

```math
x_{t+\Delta t} = \mathcal{M}(x_t, \theta, \epsilon)
```

Modern atmospheric process modeling spans four distinct architectural paradigms:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                       The Process Modeling Spectrum                                              │
├─────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────┤
│  Pure Learned Dynamics  │  Generative Diffusion   │  Hybrid Physics + Neural   │  Classical Numerical Solvers    │
│  (GraphCast, Pangu)     │  (GenCast)              │  (NeuralGCM)               │  (OpenIFS, CY48R1)              │
├─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Deterministic GNN/TF  │ • Probabilistic SDE/ODE │ • Differentiable dycore    │ • Discretized Navier-Stokes /   │
│ • State-to-state map    │ • Multi-ensemble spread │   (hydrostatic primitive)  │   primitive equations           │
│ • Optimized for MSE/MAE │ • Calibrated extremes   │ • Learned sub-grid physics │ • Hand-tuned physical parameter-│
│ • Blur at long lead     │ • Sharp fine structures │ • Exact mass/tracer cons.  │   izations                      │
│ • Fast, autoregressive  │ • Denoising reverse SDE │ • Multi-decade stability   │ • High compute, zero ML drift   │
└─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Pure Learned Dynamics (Deterministic Autoregressive)**:
   - Evaluates $x_{t+\Delta t} = f_\theta(x_t, x_{t-\Delta t})$. No explicit physics equations; entirely trained on historical reanalyses (ERA5).
   - *Strengths*: Highly computationally efficient (< 1 minute per 10-day forecast); beats operational HRES on deterministic point-verification scores (RMSE, ACC).
   - *Weaknesses*: Deterministic $L_1/L_2$ regression leads to spatial over-smoothing ("blurring") at long lead times, unphysical energy dissipation, and potential drift off the atmospheric attractor when unrolled over long horizons.
2. **Generative / Diffusion Process Models (Stochastic Ensembles)**:
   - Formulates forecast evolution as conditional score-based generation: $p(x_{t+\Delta t} \mid x_{t}, x_{t-\Delta t})$.
   - Uses reverse diffusion steps on a multi-mesh graph to generate diverse, realistic atmospheric trajectories without spatial blurring.
   - *Strengths*: Generates calibrated 50-member ensemble distributions; models extreme weather tails, tropical cyclone intensities, and localized wind extremes far better than deterministic regression.
   - *Weaknesses*: Stochastic sampling requires multiple reverse denoising steps per forecast horizon (computationally heavier than single-pass regression).
3. **Hybrid Differentiable Physics + Neural Parameterizations**:
   - Decomposes dynamics into resolved large-scale continuum mechanics and parameterized sub-grid processes:
     $$\frac{\partial X}{\partial t} = \mathcal{D}_{\text{dycore}}(X) + \mathcal{N}_\theta(X, \Phi)$$
   - A differentiable numerical solver integrates the primitive fluid dynamics equations (vorticity, divergence, hydrostatic pressure, thermodynamics), strictly enforcing physical conservation laws (mass, moisture, energy). A neural network parameterizes unresolved sub-grid physical tendencies (clouds, convection, radiative transfer, turbulence).
   - *Strengths*: Long-term physical stability (multi-decade climate simulations without climate drift); exact conservation; competitive with ECMWF HRES and ENS while using orders of magnitude less compute than classical GCMs.
   - *Key takeaway*: Directly solves the fundamental challenge: *"How do I combine known physical governing equations with learned data-driven components?"*
4. **Classical Solvers & Direct Observation Dynamics**:
   - Classical solvers (OpenIFS) use numerical discretization of the primitive Navier-Stokes equations with rule-based empirical parameterizations.
   - Direct observation world models (GraphDOP) skip gridded state dynamics entirely, rolling forward directly in a latent space initialised from observations.

### 3.4 Mathematical Formulations & Architectural Blueprints

#### 1. NeuralGCM: Differentiable Dynamical Coupling

NeuralGCM models the 3D atmosphere through the hydrostatic primitive equations split into a resolved dynamical core and an unresolved neural parameterizer:

```math
\frac{d X}{d t} = \mathcal{D}(X) + \mathcal{P}_\theta(X, \Phi)
```

- $X = (\zeta, \delta, T, q, p_s)$: Atmospheric state consisting of relative vorticity $\zeta$, divergence $\delta$, temperature $T$, specific humidity $q$, and surface pressure $p_s$.
- $\mathcal{D}(X)$: Differentiable spectral dynamical core (implemented in JAX as **Dinosaur**). Operates via spherical harmonic transforms, computing horizontal advection, Coriolis forces, pressure gradients, and adiabatic expansion exactly:
  ```math
  \frac{\partial \zeta}{\partial t} = -\nabla \cdot (\zeta + f)\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{k} \cdot \nabla \times \left(RT \nabla \ln p_s + \dot{\sigma}\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial \sigma}\right)
  ```
- $\mathcal{P}_\theta(X, \Phi)$: Neural network parameterization module (operating on column levels or localized patches) parameterized by weights $\theta$ and static surface properties $\Phi$ (orography, land fraction):
  - Ingests vertical profiles of atmospheric state and computes tendency increments $\Delta X_{\text{physics}}$ for sub-grid processes: convective precipitation, boundary layer turbulence, radiative cooling, and cloud formation.
- **End-to-End Multistep Unrolled Training**:
  - The hybrid ODE solver is integrated across $K$ time steps using differentiable Runge-Kutta / semi-implicit schemes:
    ```math
    \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k \left\| X_{t_k}^{\text{sim}} - X_{t_k}^{\text{ERA5}} \right\|_2^2
    ```
  - Because the dynamical core is fully differentiable, gradients $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$ flow through both the physical dynamics and the neural parameterizations over multi-day rollouts, preventing divergence and spurious oscillations.

#### 2. GenCast: Score-Based Diffusion on Spherical Multi-Mesh Graphs

GenCast generates future atmospheric state increments $\Delta x = x_{t+\Delta t} - x_t$ given context $c = (x_t, x_{t-\Delta t})$ by reversing a continuous stochastic differential equation (SDE):

```math
d x_\tau = f(x_\tau, \tau) d\tau + g(\tau) d w_\tau
```

- Forward SDE perturbs clean state increment $x_0 \sim p_0(x \mid c)$ into Gaussian noise $x_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\max}^2 I)$ as reverse time $\tau$ goes from $0 \to 1$.
- Denoising score matching network $s_\theta(x_\tau, \tau, c)$ is trained to approximate $\nabla_{x_\tau} \log p_\tau(x_\tau \mid c)$:
  ```math
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{score}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau, x_0, \epsilon}\left[ \lambda(\tau) \left\| s_\theta(x_\tau, \tau, c) + \frac{x_\tau - x_0}{\sigma_\tau^2} \right\|^2 \right]
  ```
- **Graph Multi-Mesh Backbone**:
  - Encodes 0.25° lat-lon grid variables to a multi-level icosahedral mesh hierarchy ($M_0$ to $M_6$).
  - Evaluates message passing across hierarchical mesh edges to model long-range teleconnections and synoptic waves without distortion at polar singularities.
  - Decodes denoised representation back to the 0.25° atmospheric state.

#### 3. GraphCast: Autoregressive Multi-Mesh GNN Simulator

GraphCast directly maps 2 consecutive time steps to the next step:

```math
x_{t+6h} = x_t + \mathcal{G}_\theta(x_t, x_{t-6h})
```

- **Encoder**: Bipartite GNN mapping grid nodes to icosahedral multi-mesh nodes $V_M$.
- **Processor**: 16 layers of homogeneous message passing on a refined multi-mesh with 6 hierarchical spatial scales:
  ```math
  v_i^{(l+1)} = \operatorname{Update}\left(v_i^{(l)}, \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \operatorname{Message}\left(v_i^{(l)}, v_j^{(l)}, e_{ij}\right)\right)
  ```
- **Decoder**: Bipartite GNN mapping multi-mesh nodes back to the 0.25° equirectangular grid.
- **Autoregressive Rollout Loss**: Trained over up to 12 autoregressive steps (72 hours) with gradient backpropagation through time.
